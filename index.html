Case Study Breakdown — How We Think

How the Kiki Challenge Made “In My Feelings” a Global Hit

In 2018, Drake’s In My Feelings went from an album track to one of the biggest songs in the world after creator Shiggy posted himself dancing to the “Kiki, do you love me?” section. It's the clearest example of the model we build campaigns around — so here's our breakdown of it.

01 — The Catch Point

A single conversational, rhythmic hook — “Kiki, do you love me?” — short and memorable enough to carry an entire video on its own.

02 — The Format

A dance anyone could recreate with no skills, equipment, or production — later evolving into the iconic moving-car visual variant that made it instantly recognizable.

03 — Who We Activate

It started with one creator, spread through everyday participants, then picked up celebrity social proof — Will Smith, Ciara, Kevin Hart, Odell Beckham Jr.

04 — How It Spread

Song → Social Format → Audience Creates Content → More Audience Discovers Song. Over half a million #InMyFeelingsChallenge clips hit Instagram by July 2018, and the track reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100.

“Don’t just promote the song. Create the moment people want to be part of.”

Case Study Breakdown — How We Think

How “HOT TO GO!” Turned a Dance Move Into a Global Hit

Chappell Roan’s HOT TO GO! was built around a simple cheer-style sequence that spells out its own title. By 2024, audiences were performing it together at festivals and concerts, and the song climbed to No. 4 in the UK more than a year after its original release. It's the clearest example of why we treat choreography as a distribution mechanism, not just a creative decision — so here's our breakdown of it.

01 — The Catch Point

A recognizable, learnable hook — a cheer-style sequence spelling out the song’s own title, inspired by the communal feel of “Y.M.C.A.” Simple enough for a crowd to pick up in seconds.

02 — The Format

Impressive enough to watch, achievable enough to copy. The movement lands precisely on the beat and lyric, giving non-dancers something they could actually reproduce while still looking exciting performed by a professional.

03 — Who We Activate

We seed in waves — a professional dancer shows what the movement can look like, a creator shows how it adapts, a non-dancer proves anyone can join in, and a larger creator adds social proof.

04 — How It Spread

Choreography → Attention → Imitation → UGC → New Audiences → Song Discovery. Audiences carried the movement from festivals into TikToks and Reels, and the song reached No. 4 in the UK more than a year after release.

“The dance isn’t the final product. The song is — choreography is just the bridge.”
